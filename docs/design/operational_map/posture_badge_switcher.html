Posture badge and posture switcher

Staff map, formation counters. All frames are 1:1 pixels at 1440×900 (the counter is the accepted hex tile, R 21 × 1.35).

The need

  • Says: the posture of every own formation – march (100 % move points, avoids contact), advance (75 %, attacks what it meets), defend (50 %, holds and fights from position; GDD "Orders and postures").
  • Today: the posture is visible only in the bottom-bar unit strip and in the line style of an order. A formation without an order, or with the line hidden (O off, Z0), shows nothing. Switching = keys 1–3 (armed gesture) or the RMB ring; no click target on the map.
  • New: (1) a badge on the tile, always readable, shape-coded (not colour-only); (2) a one-click switch that works for a selected formation and for one with an issued order (SetPostureCommand keeps the destination, the route is re-planned; free re-aim in the issuing turn, TASK-154).
  • Data: OperationalPosture id, move-point %, legal postures for the formation. Must not hide: the order line start, studs, designation label, the contact ellipse.
  • States: idle · hover · selected without order · selected with order · disabled (enemy turn, modal, slide, strike mode, enemy / estimated counter) · stack · Z0.

Glyph set (shape carries the meaning; same in the badge, switcher and unit strip)

March = double chevron (dashed line), Advance = spear (solid line), Defend = arc over a bar (dotted/arc line as in TASK-092d). Same grammar as the order line, so the badge and the line agree. No new colours: the badge is ui.text on a dark disc; colour is never the only channel. Enemy counters show no badge (posture unknown under fog; est counters stay 55 % with "?").

Variant A – Badge + strip under the selected counter (accepted)

Mouse-first. Closest to existing parts: the badge sits on the tile base (lower-left, free of the right-hand label); the selected formation grows a three-segment strip in the chamfered plate style of the cut-corner buttons, icon + name on each segment, so it is fully mouse-driven; keys 1 / 2 / 3 stay only as optional shortcuts (named in the tooltip). One click = SetPostureCommand (or arms the posture when there is no order yet).

Variant C – The badge is the control (click cycles, hover fans out)

Minimal: nothing is added to the map beyond the badge. On the selected tile the badge grows to Ø 24 px and becomes a button: click = next posture (march → advance → defend → march), Shift-click / wheel = previous, hover fans the three choices out as a short arc. Smallest footprint, but the hit target is small and cycling hides the options until hover.

Recommendation

A. Posture changes are a frequent, tactical decision (march to the front, advance on contact, defend on arrival) – the user asked for easy switching, so the three options should be visible and one click away at the unit, with the keys printed on them. B is the safest for map clutter but slow to reach; C is elegant but hides options and has a small click target. B can be added later on top of A at no cost (the unit strip already shows the posture).

Decisions (2026-11-16)

  1. Posture change of an issued order stays free (existing rule, SetPostureCommand keeps the order; no rules change).
  2. Badge on every own tile at Z1+.
  3. Glyph at the order-line head: left to TASK-157.
  4. Mouse first: keys 1–3 are only optional shortcuts.

New tokens

None. Everything uses ui.text, ui.muted, ui.amber, PANEL_STRONG, LINE, map.void. New geometry only: the three glyph polygons and the 18 px badge disc.
