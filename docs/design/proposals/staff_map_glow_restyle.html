Staff map restyle – minimal, modern, glow

Written for: the project owner, deciding the look of the operational map.

1 · The need

The current Z0/Z1 map (baked hillshade, forest and marsh textures, three road weights, relief, noise) is realistic but hard to read: roads, rivers, borders and the front fight with the terrain texture and with each other, and the counters are the only thing that pops. Goal: a minimal, modern look with a glow effect – near-black land, only the lines that matter drawn as thin light, the front line and the orders as the brightest things on the screen.

Geometry does not change. Coastlines, lakes, rivers, roads and borders still come from the real OSM/Copernicus import (rule "hexes are mechanics only" stays: nothing is bent to hexes). Only the rendering changes: no hillshade, no land-cover textures, fewer layers per zoom level, additive glow on the few lines that carry meaning. Counters (TASK-142 hex tiles), panels, turn bar and all accepted HUD components stay as they are – the proposal shows simplified counters only so the map can be judged in context.

Common rules (all variants)

  • Hierarchy by brightness, not by texture: front > orders > counters > borders > roads t1 > rivers/coast > everything else.
  • Colours stay the existing tokens (side.own #5b93d6, side.enemy #d76a52, ui.amber #e8b04a, road.t1, river.major, border…). New tokens listed per variant.
  • Meaning never rests on colour alone: own = rectangle, enemy = diamond, front = line with teeth towards the enemy, border = dash-dot, rail = dotted.
  • Zoom levels Z0/Z1/Z2 unchanged; the glow lowers with zoom-in (Z2: ~60 % of Z0) so close-up stays crisp.
  • Glow is one screen-space bloom (see Godot notes), not per-line blur meshes – near zero cost, stays within the 60 FPS budget.

Variant A · "Neon contour" – recommended

Land is a flat near-black plate with a barely visible country tint (5 %). The coast glows in a cool blue-cyan, major rivers and t1 roads are thin glowing filaments, the border is a quiet dash-dot, the front is a fat amber beam. No forest/relief at all – forests only reappear at Z2 as a 6 % dotted texture. Reads like a radar / HUD map.

Variant B · "Glow field"

Countries become soft luminous fields (own blue / enemy red, 17 %), so who holds what is readable at one glance without any label. Borders are bright white glowing lines, the front is the seam where the two fields meet, coast is dim. Roads and rivers recede to 30–55 %. Counters are solid, filled and glowing. More colour and "atmosphere" than A, but the tint competes a bit with the counters' own colour.

Variant C · "Signal only"

The most minimal: a flat charcoal plate, a 7 % hex grid as the only texture, borders and the front, t1 roads at 35 %. No rivers or lakes on Z0 (they appear on Z1). Glow only on the front, the route and towns. Maximum readability of the operational picture; the map stops looking like a place and becomes a diagram.

Godot notes

  • Glow: WorldEnvironment glow (bloom, HDR threshold ~1.0, intensity ~0.8, blend additive) on the staff-map viewport; the glowing lines get an emission colour > 1.0 (e.g. front colour × 2.2) in the line shader, everything else stays ≤ 1.0. One full-screen pass – counters and HUD (CanvasLayer) are not blurred, only the map layer; check on HIGH quality at 60 FPS, put the bloom behind a quality level if it costs frames.
  • Palette: extend StaffMapPalette with the new tokens and a "glow" strength per zoom level; drop hillshade (soft-light 0.8) and land-cover textures from the base texture; keep the importer data untouched.
  • Layers: reuse StaffMapLayers line meshes (one per class); per-variant differences are colour, width, emission and layer visibility per zoom – no new geometry.
  • Tilt: glow works with the 2.5D camera (screen-space), line widths stay constant under zoom as today.
