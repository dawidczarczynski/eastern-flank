# ADR 0031: Satellite colour layer – Sentinel-2 L2A composite, cached offline, committed as JPEG

- **Status**: accepted
- **Date**: 2026-11-22
- **Task**: TASK-192a (data and loaders; the rendering is TASK-192b). Builds on ADR 0018 (maps from real geography, tactical layer), ADR 0026 (real-scale vegetation), ADR 0028 (sub-hex land cover)

## Context

At real scale (ADR 0021) the far terrain and the forest canopy are painted from a handful of procedural tints per land-cover class; from a few kilometres away the map reads as flat, uniform patches. Real imagery would give the far ground its real colour variation (field mosaics, forest tones, villages, shores). The game never goes online, must work right after a clone, and the map importer is stdlib-only Python with a byte-identical re-import (ADR 0018).

## Decision

1. **Source: Copernicus Sentinel-2 Level-2A, our own summer composite.** Bottom-of-atmosphere reflectance, bands B04 / B03 / B02 at 10 m and the scene classification SCL at 20 m, from the *Sentinel-2 Collection 1 Level-2A* Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs on AWS (registry.opendata.aws/sentinel-2-l2a-cogs, bucket `sentinel-cogs` / `e84-earth-search-sentinel-data`, us-west-2), found through the Earth Search STAC API (`https://earth-search.aws.element84.com/v1`, collection `sentinel-2-c1-l2a`).
   - **Licence**: Copernicus Sentinel data are free, full and open (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 1159/2013; Sentinel Data Legal Notice, sentinels.copernicus.eu/documents/247904/690755/Sentinel_Data_Legal_Notice): use, modification and redistribution, also commercial, with the attribution **"Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data <year>"**. Checked 2026-11-22. The AWS registry asks to cite "Sentinel-2 Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs was accessed on <date> from https://registry.opendata.aws/sentinel-2-l2a-cogs". Both are in `data/maps/CREDITS` and in each layer's `colour_sources`.
   - **Rejected: EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless.** Only the 2016 mosaic is CC BY 4.0; 2018–2025 are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (commercial use only under a separate EOX licence), checked on cloudless.eox.at 2026-11-22. It is also served as JPEG web-map tiles in Web Mercator, which a stdlib importer cannot decode. Our own composite has no licence strings attached beyond the Copernicus attribution.
2. **Offline-repeatable cache** (`~/.cache/eastern-flank/maps/s2/`, outside git like DEM / OSM):
   - `s2v1_<s>_<w>_<n>_<e>.json`, the **scene manifest** of the map's bbox: STAC search in two fixed summer seasons (June–August 2024, then 2023), scene cloud cover < 20 %, the 3 least cloudy scenes per MGRS tile **and relative orbit** (a tile on a swath edge is only half seen by each orbit; picking per tile alone left a 20 km strip of the theatre without data), in priority order (map's UTM zone first, then cloud, no-data share, id). Written once; every later run and `--offline` use it, so a re-import reads the same scenes. The scene ids are written into the map (`colour_sources`) and CREDITS.
   - `<scene>/<band>_L<level>.tif`: a band cut after the data of COG overview `level` (GDAL COGs store the overviews before the full resolution: the 40 m overview of an 80 MB 10 m band is the first ~5 MB, fetched with one HTTP range request); level 0 is the whole file. A file of a finer level serves every coarser one. SCL files (~1 MB) are fetched for every candidate scene, the colour bands only for scenes the composite actually uses.
3. **Composite** (`tools/import_map_colour.py`): output pixels in the map's UTM zone, each band read from its coarsest overview not coarser than the pixel (GDAL AVERAGE overviews for the reflectance, MODE for SCL), nearest pixel. A pixel takes the first scene in manifest order whose SCL is clear ground (2 dark area, 4 vegetation, 5 not vegetated, 6 water) – but a scene that would add fewer than min(2 % of the pixels, 250 000) is skipped in this pass (SCL flags stray bright fields as cloud, and each extra scene is a date seam and a band download: Suwałki went from 6 scenes to 1); pixels no scene sees clear take the first scene with any data (the already used scenes first, so a few cloudy pixels do not pull in another scene's bands); pixels with no data at all take the mean colour. A scene in another UTM zone (the theatre's east edge, zone 35) is reprojected point by point, only for the pixels still open.
4. **L2A -> sRGB**: reflectance = DN × scale + offset from the STAC metadata (1e-4 and −0.1 for Collection 1, which harmonises the processing-baseline-04.00 offset); display value = sRGB transfer of 2.6 × reflectance, with a soft exponential shoulder above 0.8 (bright roofs and sand do not clip), 8 bit, per channel. **Haze and season**: L2A is already corrected for the atmosphere, so no haze is added (aerial perspective is the renderer's job, TASK-192b); summer scenes give a mid / late-summer look (green forest and meadows, a mix of ripe and harvested fields), matching the field crop mix of `LookPreset.fields()`. No other grading here: the battle colour grade (ColorGrade) applies on top.
5. **Resolution and storage.**
   - **Tactical maps**: 10 m pixels (Sentinel-2's native resolution), over the map's hexes (`HexLayout.bounds()`): ceil(extent / 10 m) pixels each way. One baseline JPEG (YCbCr 4:2:0, Annex K tables at quality 85, integer colour conversion and DCT: the same input gives the same bytes) in `ImportedMap.colour_jpg` with `colour_cell_m`, `_width`, `_height`, `_origin_easting` / `_northing` (centre of the north-west pixel) and `colour_sources`.
   - **Tactical layer** (the theatre, ADR 0018): 40 m pixels (the bands' 40 m overview; at 10 m the theatre would be 950 M pixels and ~150 MB). Geometry in `layer.tres`; one JPEG window per chunk (`TacticalChunk.colour_*`), cut from one global raster at the chunk's hex edges like the land-cover windows, each one pixel wider to the east and south than its share so that a bilinear sample never needs two windows (the overlapping pixels are equal in the raw raster; after JPEG they differ by a few levels at most). Windows are decoded only when sampled.
   - JPEG, not PNG: satellite imagery compresses ~6× better as JPEG at quality 85 than as lossless PNG, and Godot decodes it with `Image.load_jpg_from_buffer` (no import step, no `.import` files). The encoder is ~250 lines of stdlib Python.
6. **Size budget**: at most 1 MB per tactical map and 20 MB for the theatre layer; the whole colour data under `data/maps` stays under 25 MB (`data/maps` was 30 MB before). Measured 2026-11-22: 19.7 MB in all, see "Sizes". A 20 m theatre layer would be ~4× that, so it would need a new budget decision.
7. **Opt-in import.** `tools/import_map.py --colour` (tactical or layer profile; `--colour-cell`, `--colour-quality`, `--s2-manifest` for tests and pinned scene lists). Without it the importer writes exactly what it wrote before. **Loaders** (pure core, no rendering): `ColourRaster` (decoded image + metres frame, bilinear `sample`, clamped to the edge), `ImportedMap.has_colour_layer()` / `colour_raster()` / `sample(x_m, z_m)`, `TacticalLayer.has_colour_layer()` / `sample(x_m, z_m)` (metres east / south of tile (0, 0)'s centre). Maps and layers without the fields load as before (`has_colour_layer()` false). No rule, command, AI or save reads the colour; it uses no `GameState.rng`.

## Consequences

- (+) Real colour for the far terrain and the canopy, from open data with a simple attribution; the game still works offline right after a clone.
- (+) The same cache and manifest give byte-identical layers; a new import never silently picks other scenes.
- (−) A first import downloads ~250 MB per used 10 m scene (whole band files; Suwałki: one scene) and, for the theatre, the 40 m overview prefixes of the used scenes plus ~0.4 MB per candidate SCL; the theatre colour adds ~25 min to the layer import (35 min in all, pure-Python compositing, reprojection of the zone-35 scenes and JPEG).
- (−) Composites of several dates show seams where the scene changes (harvest, water level, haze: visible as tile-shaped blocks in the theatre, e.g. a brighter block in Belarus); small clouds the SCL misses (cumulus dots, cloud edges) stay in the picture, a few in the east of the theatre. Pixels are nearest-sampled, so a sub-pixel misregistration of up to half a pixel remains.
- (−) 40 m is coarse near the camera on theatre battles; TASK-192b must fade it to the splat textures up close. A finer theatre layer is a `--colour-cell` and a size-budget decision.
- (−) JPEG windows differ by a few levels at chunk borders (lossy, each window encoded on its own).
