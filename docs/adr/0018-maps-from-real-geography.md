# ADR 0018: Maps from real geography – sources, scales, projection, classification, offline import

- **Status**: accepted
- **Date**: 2026-10-31
- **Task**: TASK-087 (extends [ADR 0011](0011-third-party-assets.md))

## Context

The vision (GDD "Vision decisions") wants battles fought on the real terrain of the eastern flank: operational and tactical maps are generated from real map data, not from the noise generator (`MapGenerator`). Until now every map came from `TerrainField` noise; the core had no way to load a map, and `TerrainSurface` drew a hand-made grid without a field as flat tiles. TASK-087 step 1 asked the human to pick data sources, scales, a projection and the classification; step 2 builds the first importer and one real sample.

## Decision

The human's decisions (2026-10-31), with the options that were on the table:

### Sources and licences

| Option | Coverage | Licence | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Copernicus GLO-30 DEM** (30 m, DSM) | whole theatre (PL, LT, LV, EE, BY, UA, RU) | free with attribution | **elevation** |
| GUGiK NMT (Polish national DTM, 1 m / 5 m) | Poland only | free (Polish open-data terms) | not now – no Baltics/Belarus; may refine Polish maps later |
| **OpenStreetMap** via the Overpass API | whole theatre | ODbL 1.0 (attribution, share-alike of the derived database) | **land cover** (land use, natural areas, water; roads and waterways fetched for TASK-108) |

- The raw downloads go into a **cache outside git** (`~/.cache/eastern-flank/maps/`, `--cache=` overrides): the original Copernicus COG tiles (`dem/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N<lat>_00_E<lon>_00_DEM.tif`) and the raw Overpass JSON (`osm/osm_<s>_<w>_<n>_<e>.json`, the map's bbox + 500 m). Only the generated hex maps are committed (`data/maps/<id>.tres`). **The game never goes online.**
- Attribution lives in `data/maps/CREDITS` (both sources, the ODbL share-alike statement for the derived hex data, and per map: inputs, download date, rebuild command), in every map's `sources` field and in the README "Credits" section. `tests/unit/test_real_map_battle.gd` (`test_maps_are_credited`) fails when a map in `data/maps/` lacks the attribution or is not listed in CREDITS – the counterpart of ADR 0011's `test_third_party_assets.gd` (the credits.json manifest stays for `assets/third_party/`; map data is not an asset file there).

### Scales

| Option | Hex | Map | Size | Tiles (default map 12×10 = 120) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **250 m** (a company frontage) | **30×24** | **7.5 × 5.2 km** | **720** |
| B | 200 m | 24×20 | 4.8 × 3.5 km | 480 |
| C | 500 m (battalion) | 20×16 | 10 × 6.9 km | 320 |

**A** is the tactical scale. "250 m" is the distance between neighbouring hex centres (flat-to-flat), so one world unit (`TerrainField.HEX_SIZE` = 1, centre to corner) is 250 / √3 ≈ 144.3 m and rows are 216.5 m apart (24 rows ≈ 5.2 km, not 6 km). The operational hex will be about **5 km** (TASK-092, documented only): a battle map covers about 1.5 × 1 operational hexes – the contact hex and a half ring around it. Performance (TASK-072): 6× the tiles of the default battle; on the RX 580 the screenshot run cut grass density to 0.89 to stay in the instance budget, and an AI-vs-AI match (16 turns) takes ~5 s headless. A larger map needs TASK-072's work first.

### Projection

One local metric projection per theatre: **UTM** (transverse Mercator, WGS84), zone of the map centre – **34N (EPSG:32634) for the Suwałki Gap**. The importer projects with the Krüger series (stdlib, mm-level within a zone). The hex ↔ geography mapping is stored with the map: `crs`, `origin_easting` / `origin_northing` (centre of tile (0, 0), whole metres), `hex_spacing_m`; world x grows east, world z south (`ImportedMap.easting / northing / projected_to_tile`), and `bbox_wgs84` for reference.

### Classification

Each map is covered by a lattice of sample points every 25 m (~70 per hex). Each point gets the DEM height (bilinear between pixel centres) and an OSM cover class; areas are closed ways and multipolygon relations (rings assembled from all member ways, filled even-odd), and where areas overlap the higher class wins:

| Class | OSM tags | Terrain today | TASK-108 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.` unmapped | – | PLAINS / HILLS | open ground (mostly fields in rural Poland) |
| `G` grass | `landuse=meadow/grass/village_green/recreation_ground/cemetery/brownfield/greenfield/quarry/landfill`, `natural=grassland/heath/scrub` | PLAINS / HILLS | open |
| `A` fields | `landuse=farmland/orchard/vineyard/allotments/plant_nursery/greenhouse_horticulture` | PLAINS / HILLS | **fields** |
| `U` urban | `landuse=residential/commercial/industrial/retail/farmyard/garages/construction/railway/military` | PLAINS / HILLS | **urban** (villages = farmyards + residential) |
| `F` forest | `landuse=forest`, `natural=wood` | FOREST | forest |
| `M` marsh | `natural=wetland` | PLAINS / HILLS | **marsh** |
| `W` water | `natural=water/bay`, `water=*`, `waterway=riverbank/dock`, `landuse=reservoir/basin` | WATER | water |

- **Dominant cover per hex**: the points of a hex are grouped into water / forest / open; the largest group wins (ties prefer open, then forest – passable ground over water). The map also stores the dominant fine class per hex (`cover_rows`) for TASK-108; no rule reads it yet.
- **Roads and rivers** (TASK-108): `highway=motorway … track` and `waterway=river/stream/canal` lines are already in the cached Overpass extract; v1 does not rasterise them. Wide rivers mapped as areas are water already.
- **Elevation per hex** = DEM mean. Copernicus is a surface model (trees, buildings), so the mean uses the hex's open points when at least a quarter of it is open; a mostly forested hex takes the mean minus a 15 m canopy (`--canopy`).
- **Hills from local relief**: an open hex whose relief (90th − 10th percentile of its heights) is ≥ **15 m** (`--hills-relief`) is HILLS (Suwałki sample: 131 of 720 hexes; 12 m gave 193, 18 m gave 82). Forest stays forest.
- **Game elevation**: one water plane at 0 in the game, so water hexes get −0.4; land = 0.12 (`land_min`) + (metres − lowest land hex) × vertical scale, the scale = 1.5× true scale (`--exaggeration`; 2× made the Suwałki lake shores ~1.1 units high cliffs, because Lake Szurpiły lies 36 m above the map's lowest land), reduced if the highest hex would exceed 2.2 (`hills_max`; the camera ray starts at 3.0). Lakes at different real heights all sit at the one water plane; their banks become slopes.

### Core

- `ImportedMap` (`src/core/data/imported_map.gd`, Resource): metadata above, `terrain_rows` (one character per tile: P/F/H/W), `cover_rows`, `elevation_m` (real metres), `elevation` (game units), `sources`. `ImportedMap.find(id)` loads `data/maps/<id>.tres`; `to_grid()` builds a `HexGrid` (offset layout like `MapGenerator`) or returns null for malformed data (`problem()`).
- `HexGrid.authored_elevation`: set by `to_grid()`. `TerrainSurface` with `field == null` then blends the three tile centres around a point (smoothstep-weighted barycentric: passes through every centre, flat around it, continuous across edges) instead of flat tiles; `MapGenerator.force_terrain` keeps a land tile's real elevation when it stays land (spawn tiles), only land ↔ water changes are clamped into the generator's bands. Hand-made grids and generated maps are unchanged.
- `BattleSetup.map_id` names either `generated_river` or an imported map; `Scenario.from_setup` loads it. `map_region` (a cut of a big map) stays for TASK-094.
- Sample: `data/maps/suwalki.tres` – real data, Suwałki Landscape Park around Szurpiły (54.235 N, 22.88 E), played by `Scenario.suwalki_battle` (`tools/run.sh --scenario=suwalki_battle`).

### Tool

`tools/import_map.py` – Python 3.8 stdlib only (own GeoTIFF reader for deflate + floating-point predictor tiles, own UTM projection, scanline polygon fill; no GDAL/numpy/shapely). Optional dependencies: none required; network only for the first download (urllib). Deterministic: elements are processed sorted by (class, type, id), every number is written with fixed decimals – the same cache gives a byte-identical `.tres` (tested on the committed synthetic fixtures `tools/import_map_fixtures/` → `tests/fixtures/maps/sample_map.tres`; the real Suwałki import was also re-run offline and compared). Python tests: `tools/test_import_map.py`, run by `tools/check.sh`.

## Consequences

- (+) Battles can be fought on real terrain; the importer runs offline from the cache, so maps are reproducible and reviewable as data (`terrain_rows` diff by row).
- (+) No new runtime dependency or network access in the game; the core stays pure (`ImportedMap` is a Resource, `to_grid()` pure).
- (+) TASK-108 finds fields / urban / marsh per hex already stored, roads and rivers in the cache.
- (−) ODbL share-alike: the derived hex data of every map is offered under the ODbL (stated in CREDITS); a re-download gets today's OSM, so a rebuilt map can differ from the committed one.
- (−) One water plane: lakes at different real heights are all drawn at 0, and land is mapped with one linear scale per map – a map with lakes high above its lowest ground gets steep banks (tune `--exaggeration`).
- (−) The DSM canopy correction is a heuristic; GUGiK's DTM would be exact for Poland.
- (−) Larger maps cost frame rate and AI time (TASK-072).

## Amendment: Operational maps (TASK-092a, 2026-11-07)

The operational theatre map `data/maps/suwalki_theatre.tres` (GDD "Theatre", "Operational map") is made by the same importer with `--profile=operational`; `tactical` stays the default and its output is byte-identical to before (the `sample_map.tres` golden test is unchanged).

- **Profile `operational`**: hex 5000 m, sample lattice **250 m** (~350 points per hex; `--sample=` overrides), DEM **Copernicus GLO-90** (`--dem=glo30|glo90`; COG tiles `Copernicus_DSM_COG_30_N<lat>_00_E<lon>_00_DEM.tif` from `copernicus-dem-90m`, same cache folder; the GeoTIFF reader takes the tile size from the file, GLO-90 is 1200 × 800 pixels at 50–60 N), lines densified every 50 m. The `sources` field names GLO-90.
- **Classification** (first match wins, shares of the hex's sample points; CLI options): water ≥ 0.50 → WATER; urban ≥ 0.15 → URBAN; forest ≥ 0.50 → FOREST; marsh ≥ 0.25 → MARSH; relief (90th − 10th percentile of the open heights) ≥ **35 m** → HILLS; otherwise PLAINS (never FIELDS – farmland and grass are not downloaded, unmapped = plains). Urban = `landuse` residential / commercial / industrial / retail / farmyard / garages / construction / railway – **without `military`** (the big training grounds are mostly forest and heath and would make whole 5 km hexes urban). Tuned on the real import: only the hills threshold changed, the GDD's 60 m gave 20 hills hexes (0.5 % of the land); 50 / 45 / 40 / 35 / 30 / 25 m gave 69 / 142 / 237 / **368** / 536 / 822 (1.7 / 3.4 / 5.7 / **8.8** / 12.9 / 19.7 %). 35 m lies safely inside the 5–25 % target and puts the hills on the moraine uplands (Sambia, the Masurian and Suwałki lakelands, the Lithuanian / Ashmyany uplands), not on the Kaliningrad and Neman lowlands.
- **Roads and rivers**: roads = `highway` motorway / trunk / primary / secondary and their `_link`s; rivers = `waterway` river / canal (no streams, named or not). The corner-clip length scales with the hex: `CLIP_M × hex / 250` = 800 m at 5 km.
- **Tiled download**: the operational Overpass query asks only for what the profile reads plus `natural=coastline` ways and `boundary=administrative` + `admin_level=2` ways (for TASK-113). The map's bbox + 500 m is split on a fixed **0.5° × 0.5°** grid aligned to multiples of 0.5° (`--tile-deg=`), one cache file per tile (`osm/op1_<lat>_<lon>_<deg>.json`; `op1` is the query version, a changed query never reuses old tiles), so neighbouring maps share tiles. A tile is retried 3 times (pauses 60 / 120 / 240 s); an answer that is not complete JSON or whose `remark` reports a runtime error is not cached; a re-run continues with the missing tiles. `--offline` never downloads and names a missing tile.
- **Idempotence of tiling**: tiles are loaded one at a time (one tile's JSON in memory). An area or line that crosses a tile edge comes complete in every tile it touches (Overpass returns whole ways and relations), so it is rasterised again; the lattice fill only *raises* a point to a higher class (`max`), which gives the same result whatever the order and however often an area comes; road / river links are a set and coastlines are keyed by way id. Test: the synthetic operational fixture imported as one tile and as four tiles is byte-identical (`tests/fixtures/maps/sample_op_map.tres`, golden).
- **Sea from the coastline**: OSM has no sea polygons. `natural=coastline` ways are rasterised as barriers on the lattice (walked every quarter step, so the marks form an 8-connected chain that a 4-connected fill cannot cross) and the sea is filled 4-connected from seed points (`--sea-seed=lat,lon`, repeatable; theatre defaults: 55.30 N 20.60 E in the open Baltic west of the Curonian Spit and 55.10 N 21.05 E in the Curonian Lagoon). A seed on land (left of the nearest coastline segment – OSM draws coastlines with land on the left), on the coast or off the map is an error. Lakes and lagoon polygons stay water as before. The real import filled 57 722 points (≈ 3 600 km²).
- **One UTM zone for the whole map**: EPSG:32634 (zone 34N, the zone of the centre) also east of 24 E (Vilnius, Lida). The Krüger series stays mm-accurate 4.4° off the central meridian; the scale factor there is ~1.0006 (+0.06 %) and grid north is ~3.5° off true north – both irrelevant at 5 km hexes, and one zone keeps one rectangular grid.
- **The theatre map**: 68 × 64 hexes centred on 54.20 N **22.888 E** (0.012° west of the GDD's 22.90 E so that Kaliningrad is 2 hexes inside the west edge; Lida and Kaliningrad are the two places closest to an edge, 2 hexes). Result: plains 2 569, forest 1 018, hills 368, water 181 (4.2 %), urban 160, marsh 56; 2 659 hexes with a road link, 2 147 river hexes; all 13 listed places on URBAN hexes.
- **Measured** (dev machine, WSL2): 72 OSM tiles, 1.85 GB of Overpass JSON (6 KB for open sea to 69 MB per tile, typically 40–170 s each, the public server refused none in the final 6-tile run), first download ≈ 1 h 47 min in total (66 tiles 2026-11-02 in ~1 h 42 min, the last 6 on 2026-11-07 in 5 min); DEM 24 GLO-90 tiles, 84 MB, ~10 s. Cache for the theatre ≈ 1.9 GB. Offline import **4 min 22 s**, peak memory (max RSS) **0.90 GB**; the `.tres` is 79 KB. In the game: load 8 ms, `to_grid()` ~60 ms (≤ 1 s tested), +1.5 MB of static memory per grid; `HexGrid.path_to` with an unlimited budget across the map takes ~0.24 s (tile (0, 0) is open Baltic, so the baseline runs from the first connected land tile (0, 13) to (67, 63), 95 steps) – the baseline TASK-092c's planner (budget 30 ms) must beat.
- **Known limitation**: the `admin_level=2` *ways* in the cache cover the Polish–Belarusian and Lithuanian–Belarusian borders and the sea borders, but most of the Polish–Russian and Lithuanian–Russian (Neman) borders are untagged member ways of the country relations and are missing. TASK-113 needs a small extra borders download (`relation["boundary"="administrative"]["admin_level"="2"](bbox); way(r)(bbox);`) or the relations' member ways – a new query version, not a re-download of the land cover.

### Countries (TASK-113)

Every land hex of an operational map knows its country (`ImportedMap.countries`, ISO codes in the `--countries` order, and `country_rows`, one character per tile: `0`-`9` = index, `.` = none; `country_at(coord)` reads them). Tactical maps have none.

- **Method: seed fill, not relations.** `--countries=PL:54.10,22.93,LT:...` gives each country a seed point inside it (the operational profile defaults to Suwałki, Kaunas, Grodno and Kaliningrad). The country-border ways are rasterised on the 250 m sample lattice as **barrier points** (`border_barrier`, a *supercover*: every lattice cell a segment passes through is marked, so the barrier is a 4-connected chain and neither a 4- nor an 8-connected fill leaks through a diagonal border). A **4-connected flood fill** from each seed gives every reached point its country; a barrier point takes the country of the **majority of its 4 neighbours** (ties: the first country in `--countries` order). Water points are filled like land, so a lake or the sea on a border is split by the border line; the hex country counts only land points.
- **Hex country** = the country with the most **land** (non-water) sample points in the hex (ties: first in order). A hex classified WATER gets none (it may still hold some land points), so "no country on a water tile" holds. A seed on the border line or off the map, two seeds in one region (a gap in the border line; the error names both countries) and a land hex without a country (a leak or a missing seed; the error lists the first five) all stop the import instead of writing a wrong map.
- **Why not Overpass relations.** The country relations are whole-country multipolygons (Russia, Poland, ...): downloading them is far more than a regional map needs. The border is only a line to separate seeds, so the member *ways* near the map are enough.
- **Borders-only tiles.** The `admin_level=2` ways cached by the land-cover tiles miss most of the Poland-Russia and Lithuania-Russia (Neman) borders - they are untagged member ways of the relations. A second query version `bd1` (`relation["boundary"="administrative"]["admin_level"="2"](bbox); way(r)(bbox); (._;>;); out body;`) is cached per 0.5 degree tile as `bd1_<lat>_<lon>_<deg>.json` next to the `op1` files; the land-cover tiles are not re-downloaded. The importer merges the border ways of both kinds of file by way id (no duplicates). `--offline` never downloads and a missing border tile fails naming it. The ways come back whole (not clipped), but only the ways touching the tile.
- **Drawing: the real ways, never the hexes (amendment 2026-11-07).** The per-hex country is for the rules only; the staff map does not draw hex edges. A hexagon is a unit of mechanics, the drawn map must look like the real map. The importer therefore also writes `ImportedMap.border_lines` (`border_polylines`): the border ways clipped to the map rectangle, chained end to end where exactly two ways meet (a junction of three or more ends a line), converted to **world units** (Vector2 x = world x east, y = world z south, hex size 1 - the space of `Hex.to_world`, so the view needs no conversion) and simplified with Douglas-Peucker at 0.1 hex (about 290 m), in a deterministic order (ways by id, each line from its smaller end point, lines sorted by first point). `problem()` requires at least two finite points per line. The earlier hex-edge helper (`CountryBorders.edges`) was removed. The file gains one line (`border_lines`, after `country_rows`); all other lines are unchanged.
- **Order of the fill and the line rows**: `countries`, `country_rows` and `border_lines` are written after the link rows, so a re-import of the theatre changes no other line of the file.
- **Measured** (dev machine, WSL2, 2026-11-07): the 72 border tiles of the theatre are 3.8 MB in total (typically 287 bytes to a few hundred KB; the public server needs ~36 s per tile even for an empty one, 14 attempts were retried after 502 / 504), 68 min in all; the offline import with countries takes 300 s (was ~270 s). Result: PL 1 568, LT 1 384, BY 650, RU 569 hexes; the Kaliningrad oblast is one region, no RU hex touches a BY hex, and the shortest chain of PL / LT hexes from RU to BY (the Suwalki Gap) is **16 hexes** (the real gap is ~65 km, 13 hexes; the discrete hex borders and the hex-majority rule add a few).

### Graphic layers (TASK-126, 2026-11-07)

The staff map is drawn from real geography, not from hexes (GDD "Operational map", map principle), so an operational import also writes three images of its 250 m sample lattice next to the rules grid. No rule reads them; the rules grid and every existing line of the file are unchanged (the layer lines come after `sources`; the re-imported theatre differs from the TASK-113 file only by the added lines – checked with `diff`, and tested: `build_map` with and without layers gives the same tiles).

- **Layers** (`ImportedMap.layer_*`): `water` 1-bit (the lattice's water class: lakes, reservoirs, riverbanks, the sea fill; decodes to 0 / 255), `cover` 8-bit (the `COVER_CLASSES` index, `ImportedMap.COVER_*`; on the operational map only open / urban / forest / marsh / water occur), `relief` 8-bit hillshade (Horn gradient, Lambert shading, sun 315° / 45°, flat = 180, heights × `--relief-z=4` because the theatre's slopes over 250 m are a few percent; DEM minus the 15 m canopy on forest points, as for the hex mean). Water and cover have one pixel per lattice point (row 0 north); the relief has one pixel per **2 × 2** points (500 m, `--relief-step`), its heights tent-filtered onto every second lattice point so its pixel centres stay on lattice points.
- **Storage**: grayscale PNGs (stdlib writer: water and cover unfiltered, the relief with the min-sum-of-abs adaptive filter, zlib level 9 – deterministic) as base64 `PackedByteArray("…")` text, which Godot 4.5 parses. Godot decodes them with `Image.load_png_from_buffer` (FORMAT_L8). Geo-reference: `layer_origin_easting / northing` = centre of pixel (0, 0), `layer_pixel_m`; `ImportedMap.layer_pixel_to_world` / `layer_world_rect(kind)` give the world placement.
- **Why the relief is 500 m**: Godot's text parser reads ~115 ms per MB of `.tres` text. A 250 m hillshade is 0.93 MB of PNG (1.24 MB of base64), which made the theatre load **+200 ms** (budget +150 ms); quantising it (4 levels per step: 0.58 MB) was still too slow. At 500 m it is 0.26 MB and the shading is soft anyway (bilinear filter). Unfiltered cover PNGs are 219 KB instead of 341 KB with adaptive filters. A binary sidecar file would load faster but is not a resource (export filters) – not needed now.
- **Measured** (theatre 68 × 64 hexes, layers 1 371 × 1 115 px, relief 686 × 558; dev machine, WSL2): file **655 KB** (was 90 KB); load **74 ms** (was 9 ms) + decoding the three PNGs **13 ms** = **+78 ms**; offline import 295 s (was ~300 s), peak memory 0.91 GB; two imports byte-identical. 93 752 water pixels; Wigry and Hańcza are water although their hexes are land; the Curonian Spit is a land strip of 7–13 pixels between sea and lagoon.
- **Older maps**: maps without layers (tactical maps, older files) load unchanged; `has_graphic_layers()` is false and the view keeps its hex look.

## Amendment: Tactical layer for contacts anywhere (TASK-094b, 2026-11-08)

A contact can happen at any hex of the theatre, so every battle needs a tactical (250 m) map of the real terrain around it. The theatre is 1370 × 1288 ≈ 1.76 M tactical hexes. Two options were measured (dev machine, WSL2, 4 cores, 7 GB RAM); budget **[ASSUMPTION]**: contact map ready ≤ 1.5 s, repository growth ≤ 50 MB.

| | (A) pre-imported chunked layer | (B) cut imported on demand from the cache |
|---|---|---|
| Inputs | the operational cache already on disk (72 `op1_*` OSM tiles, 24 GLO-90 tiles) | the same cache (1.9 GB) – outside git, the game never goes online, so it would have to ship |
| Offline cost | one import: **9 min 32 s**, peak RSS **1.02 GB** | none |
| Repository | **3.32 MB** (122 files: `layer.tres` + 121 chunks of 128 × 128, 2.5 MB gzipped) | 0, but **1.9 GB** of raw data to ship |
| Contact map | cold: index 0.3 ms, 4 chunks parsed + decoded + 40 × 30 grid **25 ms**, +0.8 MB memory; cached chunks 8 ms; `ContactMap.build` + `Scenario.from_setup` over 37 contacts of the `suwalki_gap` start: slowest **248 ms**, mean **162 ms** | Python importer on one cut (one 56 MB tile): **8.5 s**, 0.41 GB; a corner contact touches up to 4 tiles. In Godot, `JSON.parse` of that one tile alone takes **2.06 s** (+ 776 MB) |

**Decision: A.** B fails both budgets (time ≥ 5× over, ships 1.9 GB). A is 3.3 MB and ~0.25 s.

- **Import**: `tools/import_map.py --profile=layer` (rebuild command in CREDITS). Same inputs and sea fill as the operational profile; a **50 m** cover lattice for the whole theatre (38 M points, one byte each; polygons filled by `_scanline_fill_fast` – edges bucketed by row, spans raised by `bytes.translate`; same points as `_scanline_fill`, tested); per hex the cover classes of the ~23 lattice points of `_hex_pattern` and the DEM at the centre plus six points 110 m around it (GLO-90 pixels are ~90 m; the coordinates of the six come from a local linearisation of `from_utm`) feed the **tactical** `classify` (water / forest dominance, urban 20 %, marsh 25 %); the elevation rule (open points, or all minus the 15 m canopy) is the tactical one. Nothing per lattice point is kept besides the cover bytes, so memory stays at ~1 GB.
- **Hills**: 7 GLO-90 samples see less relief than ~70 GLO-30 samples. Calibrated on the `suwalki.tres` area (GLO-30, 15 m → 129 hills of 720): radius 80 m / 110 m and thresholds 6–15 m gave 28–268; **110 m and 10 m** give 142. Whole layer: plains 935 784, forest 564 421, urban 104 386, water 103 755, marsh 34 958, hills 21 256; 78 776 road hexes, 56 114 river hexes; 1.46 M sea lattice points.
- **Limits of the inputs**: only major roads (motorway … secondary) and rivers / canals (no streams); the DEM is GLO-90; the hand-made `suwalki.tres` sample stays the detailed one.
- **Fields (2026-11-08, follow-up)**: the `op1` tiles have no farmland / grass, so the first layer had **no FIELDS** (open land all PLAINS). The layer now also reads 72 **land-use tiles** `lu1_*` (`landuse_query`: only the A / G values of the tactical tag table – `landuse=farmland/orchard/vineyard/allotments/plant_nursery/greenhouse_horticulture` → A, `meadow/grass/…` and `natural=grassland/heath/scrub` → G; same 0.5° grid, retried, 5 s pause between downloads; **891 MB**, ~70 min on the public Overpass server, 32 transient 502 / 504 answers all recovered by the retries). They are rasterised into the same 50 m lattice with the same "highest class wins" rule, so fields / grass never cover forest, urban, marsh or water, and `classify` is unchanged: FIELDS only where farmland is the largest open class and the hex is not urban / marsh / hills (as in `suwalki.tres`; meadow and grass stay PLAINS, ADR 0019).
